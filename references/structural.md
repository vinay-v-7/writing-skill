# Structural rubric — 30 STORYSCOPE core features

**Evidence:** T1 · Russell et al., *StoryScope*, COLM 2026, arXiv:2604.03136v6. Means from Table 16; questions/options from Tables 14–15. Prescriptions and overshoot notes are craft guidance built on those means.

**Last reviewed (structural):** durable — re-review only when STORYSCOPE-class evidence updates.

**How to use:** Read during Phase C. Emit a shortlist of the 3 worst cluster offenders. Move toward the human mean and stop. Prefer subtraction on AI-elevated axes. Change few axes, deeply. Targets are distribution means, not maxima.

**Units:** `sc` = scale/ordinal points; `pp` = percentage-point prevalence gap for a named option. H = human mean, A = AI mean.

**Calibration:** (1) Move toward human mean and stop. (2) Prefer subtraction on AI-elevated axes. (3) Change few axes, deeply.

## Cluster A — AI-elevated · Thematic over-determination

### Thematic Explicitness & Moralizing
- **Q:** How explicitly does the story articulate its themes or morals? · scale 1–5 · **H/A:** 3.28 / 3.94 · gap −0.65 sc · rank 1
- **Rx:** Cut the sentence that names the lesson. If the theme only lands because one line states it, it hasn't been earned by the events.
- **Ov:** Human mean is 3.28, not 1. A story that refuses all thematic signal reads as arch and withholding.

### Moral / Philosophical Weighting
- **Q:** How heavily does the story foreground moral or philosophical questions? · **H/A:** 3.26 / 3.68 · −0.42
- **Rx:** Let the moral question be a by-product of the situation rather than its subject.
- **Ov:** Humans still average 3.26. Moral weight isn't the problem; foregrounding it is.

### Thematic Unity
- **Q:** To what extent do subplots and flourishes serve a central thematic concern? · **H/A:** 4.41 / 4.74 · −0.33
- **Rx:** Allow exactly one element that exists because it's interesting rather than because it serves the theme.
- **Ov:** Only a 0.33 gap. Incoherence is not humanity.

### Narratorial Thematic Commentary → yes
- **Q:** Does the narrator explicitly comment on themes beyond characters' perspectives? · **H/A:** 52% / 77% · −25 pp
- **Rx:** Find the paragraph where narration steps outside every character to tell us what this means, and delete it.
- **Ov:** Humans do this 52% of the time. A narrator with no perspective is its own tell.

### Dialogue Function → philosophical debate
- **Q:** What main functions does dialogue serve? · **H/A:** 34% / 59% · −25 pp
- **Rx:** Let characters talk about logistics, money, weather, old grudges — not only meaning.
- **Ov:** A third of human stories use dialogue philosophically; the issue is it being the default.

### Reference Explicitness → implicit echoes
- **Q:** Are intertextual gestures primarily explicit or diffuse? · **H/A:** 50% / 72% · −22 pp
- **Rx:** When you reach for an allusion, either name the actual thing or drop it.
- **Ov:** Paired with human-elevated “balanced mix” — target a mixture of registers.

## Cluster B — AI-elevated · Sensory & embodied performativity

### Dominant Emotional Expression → embodied
- **Q:** How are characters' emotions most commonly conveyed? · **H/A:** 38% / 81% · −42 pp · largest option gap
- **Rx:** Stop converting every feeling into a tight chest / dry throat. Roughly half should become behaviour, plain statement, or nothing.
- **Ov:** Humans embody emotion 38% of the time — eliminating it overshoots. **Regime:** short-form Pass S may still add one concrete detail; long-form craft thins embodied rendering.

### Setting as Psychological Mirror
- **H/A:** 3.58 / 4.07 · −0.49 · **Rx:** Let weather be weather. Not every rainstorm needs to coincide with grief.
- **Ov:** 3.58 is still high — reduce hit rate, don't ban pathetic fallacy.

### Environmental & Ecological Emphasis
- **H/A:** 2.83 / 3.21 · −0.38 · **Rx:** Trim nature description that isn't doing plot, character, or timing work.
- **Ov:** Small gap. Genre matters more than this number.

### Dominant Sensory Modalities → olfactory
- **H/A:** 57% / 82% · −26 pp · **Rx:** Smell detail is the most reflexive AI sensory move. Cut roughly a third.
- **Ov:** 57% of human stories still use olfactory imagery.

### Sensory Density
- **H/A:** 3.66 / 3.93 · −0.26 · **Rx:** Modest thinning. Some passages should move through space without describing it.
- **Ov:** A trim, not plain reportage.

### Depth of Interior Access
- **H/A:** 3.67 / 3.93 · −0.26 · **Rx:** Let some characters stay opaque.
- **Ov:** Human mean still 3.67. Opacity as technique, not policy.

## Cluster C — AI-elevated · Structural streamlining

### Continuity of Main Causal Chain
- **H/A:** 3.92 / 4.20 · −0.28 · **Rx:** Permit one event that doesn't cause the next.
- **Ov:** Humans still strongly causal at 3.92. One or two loose joints, not collapse.

### Spatial Granularity Level
- **H/A:** 2.27 / 2.53 · −0.26 · **Rx:** Not every room needs a floor plan.
- **Ov:** Minor; fix by deleting, not vaguer description.

### Agency in Resolution → protagonist choice
- **H/A:** 46% / 69% · −23 pp · **Rx:** About a quarter of endings driven by external fate or mixed causes.
- **Ov:** Protagonist agency still human plurality at 46% — shift the mix.

### Character Introduction → external description
- **H/A:** 30% / 52% · −22 pp · **Rx:** Stop opening with looks/habits. Prefer **in-dialogue** intro (human fingerprint uniq 21.4); avoid in-action (Kimi).
- **Ov:** See fingerprints.

### Subplot Integration → no subplots
- **H/A:** 57% / 79% · −22 pp · **Rx:** Roughly one story in five needs a genuine second thread.
- **Ov:** Most human stories (57%) still have no subplots. Adding one to every piece overshoots.

### Mode of Resolution → internal understanding
- **H/A:** 27% / 47% · −21 pp · **Rx:** Most over-used ending: protagonist understands and accepts. Resolve externally, or leave unresolved.
- **Ov:** 27% of human stories still end this way — legitimate, not house style.

### Pre-Threat Character Investment
- **H/A:** 2.76 / 2.99 · −0.23 · **Rx:** Start closer to the trouble.
- **Ov:** Small gap; cutting setup too hard costs footing.

### Opening Spatial Grounding
- **H/A:** 2.12 / 2.33 · −0.20 · **Rx:** Don't always establish place in first paragraph.
- **Ov:** Lowest priority; fix when free.

## Cluster D — Human-elevated · Intertextual richness

### Intertextual Strategy Types → explicit named reference
- **H/A:** 47% / 24% · +23 pp · rank 1 human side · **Rx:** Name the actual book, band, brand, street, film, or person. Highest-value nonfiction transfer.
- **Ov:** Name-dropping as texture is hollow. Each named thing should be one a character would plausibly know.

### Reference Explicitness → balanced mix
- **H/A:** 37% / 16% · +21 pp · **Rx:** Mix registers within one piece — some named, some echoes.
- **Ov:** “Balanced” caps itself by definition.

## Cluster E — Human-elevated · Reader engagement

### Fourth-Wall Permeability
- **H/A:** 0.67 / 0.39 · +0.28 · **Rx:** Let the telling acknowledge itself occasionally — aside about what's left out, narrator admitting a gap.
- **Ov:** Radical violations are a whole-piece commitment, not a garnish.

### Frequency of Direct Reader Address
- **H/A:** 0.28 / 0.07 · +0.21 · **Rx:** A single well-placed “you” aside is usually the entire fix.
- **Ov:** Highest overshoot risk on the board. Frequent address is a strong voice choice, not default.

## Cluster F — Human-elevated · Temporal complexity

### Depth of Recontextualization After Surprise
- **H/A:** 3.28 / 2.95 · +0.34 · **Rx:** Plant material early that changes meaning after the reveal.
- **Ov:** Every scene secretly meaning something else is a short-lived gimmick.

### Degree of Chronological Discontinuity
- **H/A:** 2.40 / 2.12 · +0.28 · **Rx:** Jump. Skip transitional material.
- **Ov:** Typical human story still moves forward in order. One or two jumps; not kaleidoscope.

### Nonlinear Framing for Delayed Disclosure
- **H/A:** 1.96 / 1.68 · +0.28 · **Rx:** Stage reveal with structure rather than narrator withholding.
- **Ov:** Both means &lt;2. Heavy fragmentation is the most over-applied axis.

### Anachrony Intensity
- **H/A:** 2.58 / 2.31 · +0.27 · **Rx:** One real flashback/flash-forward that carries weight.
- **Ov:** Dominant anachrony is a whole-piece commitment.

---

## Cluster G — Human-elevated · Narrative diversity

### Location Variety Scope
- **H/A:** 1.34 / 1.08 · +0.26 · **Rx:** Move somewhere else at least once.
- **Ov:** Two or three locations closes the gap; a tour does not.

### Dialogue-to-Narration Proportion
- **H/A:** 2.95 / 2.70 · +0.24 · **Rx:** More talking, less accounting. Convert a summarized exchange into a scene.
- **Ov:** Pairs with dialogue-function: more philosophical-debate dialogue makes things worse.

### Subplot Integration → thematically parallel
- **H/A:** 42% / 21% · +21 pp · **Rx:** When a subplot exists, let it rhyme with the main thread rather than restate it.
- **Ov:** Paired “no subplots” caps this: most human stories have none.

### Moral Polarity Toward Protagonist → ambivalent/mixed
- **H/A:** 59% / 38% · +21 pp · **Rx:** Protagonist should be wrong about something and not corrected.
- **Ov:** Ambivalence isn't absence of moral position — it's declining to resolve one the story raised.

### Dominant Emotional Expression → explicit labels
- **H/A:** 29% / 8% · +21 pp · **Rx:** “She was angry.” Allowed. Absolute “show don't tell” is itself the tell.
- **Ov:** 29%, not 100%. Seven in ten human emotional beats still indirect.
